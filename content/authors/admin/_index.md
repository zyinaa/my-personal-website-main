Hi, welcome to my homepage.

I am currently an M.S. student in Statistics at The George Washington University. My research lies at the intersection of artificial intelligence and statistical methodology, with a particular focus on large language models (LLMs), self-evolving and adaptive AI systems, statistical machine learning, sequential inference, change-point detection, and reliable and efficient AI.

My current research studies how self-evolving LLM systems can determine when to stop iterative improvement and which intermediate model or artifact should ultimately be selected. In this work, I use tools from online sequential testing, anytime-valid inference, and change-point estimation to develop statistically principled methods for improving the efficiency and reliability of self-evolving AI systems. This work is available as the preprint [*When Is Enough Enough in Self-Evolving LLM Systems?*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04756).

Before joining GWU, I received my B.Sc. in Mathematics from the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (HKUST), where I studied statistics, financial mathematics, stochastic processes, and time-series analysis. My undergraduate capstone focused on time-series modeling and portfolio optimization.

My previous research experience spans statistical learning, financial machine learning, natural language processing, and data-driven empirical research. These projects include machine-learning methods for foreign-exchange asset pricing, Chinese social-media opinion analysis using topic modeling and sentiment analysis, and applications of deep learning to high-dimensional data.

More broadly, I am interested in developing statistically grounded methods for modern AI systems, particularly methods that make large-scale learning systems more reliable, adaptive, and computationally efficient.
